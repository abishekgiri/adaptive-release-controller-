# Feasibility decision and stopping rules

**CONDITIONALLY FEASIBLE — solve named access/outcome problems first.** PATH B remains the chosen direction, but `ci-design-v2` is **not ratified**. This is a conditional assessment of a potential study, not authorization to recruit, collect data, instrument a repository, run models or rewrite the manuscript. Existing implementation effort contributes no evidence to this verdict.

There is a documented protected-job architecture and a precisely definable candidate execution outcome. There is not yet a confirmed cooperating owner, validated real release-stage outcome, complete admission frame, passive pre-approval capture route, event-accrual budget or independent evaluation cohort. These named gaps prevent an unconditional FEASIBLE verdict. They do not yet justify NOT FEASIBLE because owner-operated exports and passive observation are plausible routes that have not been tried with permission.

## Gates and stop rules

All windows and resource limits below are proposed planning limits, not ratified experiment thresholds. Any change must be justified by access/measurement evidence, before seeing model comparisons.

| Gate | Evidence needed to proceed | Stop, narrow or defer if absent |
|---|---|---|
| Cooperating access | Named owner with authority, a legitimate existing protected release operation, Level 1 semantic validation and credible Level 2 access/export route | After a later-authorized bounded six-week / 12-invitation outreach effort, no commitment: stop empirical development. Do not start an endless repository scrape |
| Meaningful target | Owner verifies job semantics, operational consequence and immutable admission-to-outcome linkage | Only ordinary CI results, gate-created failures, fabricated failures or ambiguous statuses remain: abandon this release-stage question or explicitly review a different question |
| Full request frame | Requested/approved/denied/no-start/cancelled/missing states reconciled over a fixed window | No denominator or unknown systematic loss: cannot quantify selection/coverage. Restrict to a transparent archival case description or stop prediction claims |
| Timing | Required context is actually captured while the existing gate remains held; timing uncertainty and missed fast approvals quantified | Only post-approval reconstruction or artificially extended waits available: stop the proposed passive pre-admission study; never weaken the timing contract |
| Outcome follow-up | Valid start and terminal labels; administrative and missing states separately observed, with acceptable prespecified coverage for the estimand | Missingness plausibly hides key failures and cannot be bounded or reconciled: stop binary risk claims; do not count missing/denied as success |
| Event accrual | A documented calendar/release budget can produce sufficiently many independent adverse events for the intended precision | Near-zero failure incidence or insufficient releases within resources: stop predictive comparisons. Retain descriptive rates with uncertainty if worthwhile |
| Selection | Approved/observed population is meaningful for the proposed prediction claim; severity/support/missingness can be described | No useful variation after selection or pressure to claim full-population admission benefit without identification: stop that claim. Severe selection need not invalidate a clearly bounded approved-case study |
| Prospective permission | Passive Level 2 observation or an authentic preexisting prospective archive is allowed | No passive capture and no authentic archive: stop the proposed prospective paper. Refusal of Level 3 alone does **not** stop prediction |
| Decision relevance | Owner identifies a real failure burden and a plausible use for advance risk information | An accurate warning would not matter, or only unforeseeable external errors remain: do not escalate to admission support; a measurement paper needs independent value |
| Replication/precision | Service/owner dependencies known, independent evaluation feasible, uncertainty targets set before models | More seeds, reruns or repositories sharing one release system are the only way to enlarge n: narrow claims or stop broad comparisons |
| Privacy/publication | Authorized minimization/retention and ethics determination where applicable; permission to report honest aggregate outcomes | Access requires unnecessary sensitive data or permits only favorable publications: decline that route |
| Scientific contribution | Clear distinction from existing release/defect prediction and safe-deployment analytics; defensible measurement or empirical insight | Only a familiar classifier on a tiny convenience sample with no meaningful comparison: stop positioning it as a research contribution |

If capture/label coverage is imperfect, a high aggregate percentage alone is insufficient: inspect the missing strata. A perfectly complete but uninformative one-class sample also fails predictive adequacy. Numerical coverage/precision gates should be set after an authorized data-only semantic/accrual pilot and before ratification; their current absence is a blocker to experiments, not permission to choose them after results.

## Event volume and calendar realism

There is no known number of eligible requests or protected-stage failures in current data. Do not transplant the earlier CI failure counts. Obtain owner aggregate counts over a fixed six-month window, including all exclusions and shared-service dependencies, then budget both training and untouched temporal evaluation separately.

For planning only, expected observed failures are approximately `sum_j(N_j * p_j * c_j)`, where N_j is approved started executions, p_j their failure probability and c_j the valid snapshot/label fraction. If missingness is outcome-dependent this product is only a rough accrual scenario, not an unbiased estimator; use actual joint counts when available.

Illustrations, **not study results or sufficient-sample recommendations**:

| Hypothetical failure rate | Resolved executions expected to yield 100 failures, before clustering or capture loss |
|---|---:|
| 0.5% | 20,000 |
| 1% | 10,000 |
| 5% | 2,000 |

A simple independent-binomial approximation needs about 96 adverse examples for a worst-case 95% half-width of 0.10 on recall (`1.96^2 * 0.25 / 0.10^2`). This is only one metric's illustrative precision, not a predictive-study power calculation. Calibration, paired model differences, project-level variability, training data and rare strata may require substantially more. With only 20 failures, recall changes by five percentage points per event even before clustering.

At ten approved executions/week and 2% failures, one service accrues roughly 0.2 failures/week. Six equally active services would need about 83 weeks for 100 failures, even assuming perfect capture and independent outcomes. This shows why a few monthly-release repositories may make the project impractical. Different costs, larger operators or a genuinely different, separately reviewed endpoint may change feasibility; manufacturing failures or widening the label after seeing results cannot.

Count independent services/teams/organizations and shared incidents separately from attempts. The provisional six-service/three-organization recruitment target is a coverage goal, not a minimum guaranteeing inference. Two projects cannot establish general sufficiency of simple models. A well-instrumented single-organization paper would need a bounded case-study contribution and honest limits.

## What could make a paper publishable?

No present evidence supports a submission-level empirical release-prediction paper. Publishability would require a separately assessed contribution and a suitable venue, not simply more implementation.

Potential defensible contributions include: a carefully validated prospective protected-release dataset/measurement contract; a replicated test of whether context adds value beyond scalar history at the real decision boundary; or a well-powered scoped null result showing no practically meaningful added value under specified conditions. A detailed case study could be worthwhile with unusually strong operational evidence and a clear research question, even without broad generalization.

Future prediction evaluation would need temporally valid snapshots, adequate adverse-event counts, consistent baseline information/tuning budgets, proper probability/calibration metrics and owner-relevant alert burden, uncertainty reflecting independent clusters, transparent selection and reproducibility compatible with privacy. These are requirements for a later design, **not experiments run or ratified here**.

Possible conclusions and permitted claims:

| Possible later result | Defensible claim | Claim not licensed |
|---|---|---|
| Context clearly improves prediction | Context adds measurable predictive value for the specified protected operation and observed approved population | Automatic admission saves costs or prevents incidents |
| Context helps in specified strata | Benefit is conditional on those prespecified environments/workloads and supported uncertainty | Broad superiority across software projects |
| Scalar rates match context with adequate equivalence precision | No practically meaningful contextual improvement within evaluated conditions | Simple models are generally sufficient |
| Results are imprecise or one-class | Evidence is inconclusive; report accrual/measurement limits | No context signal exists |
| Scores are predictive but operationally unhelpful | Prediction is feasible; useful admission support remains unsupported | Accurate prediction alone justifies denial |
| Engineering gate test succeeds | Tested configuration supports the recorded timing/action behavior | Real-world predictive or deployment-control effectiveness |

Prior safe-deployment work such as [Gandalf](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/an-intelligent-end-to-end-analytics-service-for-safe-deployment-in-large-scale-cloud-infrastructure/) means the novelty review must address existing operational analytics. This preliminary check is not a literature-completeness claim. Bandits should enter only if a later real feedback/reward contract makes them scientifically necessary; they are not part of the proposed first paper's identity.

## Direct answers

1. **What exactly would the paper study?** The value of pre-admission upstream CI/change/history information for predicting execution failure of a real protected release job in the existing approved, observed execution population. Cost-sensitive decision support is a subsequent stage.
2. **Outcome variable?** `Y_exec=1` for a validated started job attempt ending in failure or execution timeout; `0` for success. Denial, no-start, cancellation, supersession and missing records remain distinct, not invented labels. See [exact target](outcome-definition.md).
3. **Decision?** Approve or deny the current correctly bound protected-stage admission, within the actual run/environment scope. No canary; no claim to replace business/compliance approval.
4. **Access?** Owner-validated Level 1 metadata/exports and a Level 2 passive snapshot/outcome route for prospective prediction; separate Level 3 authority only for intervention. See [access requirements](access-requirements.md).
5. **Can the outcome be observed reliably?** The job endpoint is technically recordable, but semantics, immutable joins and completeness are UNVERIFIED for any participant. Health/incident/rollback targets require additional sources.
6. **How severe is selective labeling?** Structurally serious for full-population prediction or policy value; empirical severity unknown. It can be handled by a narrow descriptive/predictive estimand and explicit limits, not by assigning outcomes to denied requests. See [analysis](selective-label-analysis.md).
7. **Is a collaborator realistically obtainable?** Plausible, not established. There are zero confirmed participants. The bounded owner-mediated [recruitment protocol](recruitment-protocol.md) is the proposed way to test this, only after outreach authorization.
8. **Would a disposable repo help?** Yes, for engineering observability, identity, approval/denial behavior and timing in that configuration. It adds no empirical evidence for the research hypothesis and is not authorized. See [mechanism plan](mechanism-test-plan.md).
9. **What would make this publishable?** A meaningful and validated outcome, genuine pre-admission context, adequate independent event evidence, honest baseline comparisons/selection limits and a contribution distinct from known prediction/monitoring work. Current evidence is insufficient.
10. **What would make us abandon it?** No cooperating access, no valid consequential endpoint, unmeasurable timing/labels, unaffordable adverse-event accrual, unresolvable selection for the desired claim, or no research/operational value after a bounded review.

**Final choice: CONDITIONALLY FEASIBLE — solve named access/outcome problems first.** Do not ratify v2, commission models or rewrite the paper on the strength of this review. There is a plausible study to investigate, not yet an available dataset with which to conduct it.
