# Path B: target and decision feasibility

**CONDITIONALLY FEASIBLE — solve named access/outcome problems first.** Prepared 2026-09-07. This is a feasibility proposal, not ratification of `ci-design-v2`. No models, recruitment, collection, manuscript rewrite or live test is authorized by this document. The [existing v2 draft](../ci-design-v2-draft.md) remains unchanged.

The proposed first paper would ask: **Can information actually captured while an existing protected release job awaits admission predict that job's unsuccessful execution among subsequently approved requests?** Whether such predictions improve admission decisions is a later question. The present evidence does not establish that a suitable cohort, reliable outcome source or useful prediction task is available.

## Candidate outcomes

Definitions below are proposed measurement contracts, not assertions that participants already record these data. “Recorded” means an owner-validated source with complete coverage and identity linkage, not merely an API field with a suggestive name.

| Candidate | Exact proposed definition and observation horizon | Source and recording reliability | Consequence and relation to deployment | Effect of approval/denial on observation | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protected release-job execution failure | For the named admitted job attempt that starts, terminal execution conclusion `failure` or execution `timed_out`, versus `success`; administrative/nonterminal states separate | Actions job metadata plus owner-validated workflow semantics and an execution witness; available after terminal completion and actual collector receipt. Reconcile missing/deleted jobs and job/step inconsistencies | Operational failure of a release/publish/deploy stage; may be runner setup, credentials or upload failure before any release side effect. Not necessarily user impact | Denial prevents this execution label; approval can still be followed by cancellation or failure to start | **Primary candidate**, conditional on semantic and coverage validation |
| Entire release-workflow failure | Terminal unsuccessful conclusion of all jobs in a specified release workflow attempt | Workflow metadata after termination; label can include unrelated tests, cleanup or gate rejection | A pipeline aggregate, potentially just another CI result; weak connection to the protected operation | Denial itself may cause unsuccessful workflow completion, making the label action-created | Reject as primary; preserve diagnostic status only |
| Rollback | An explicit executed operation restoring a named environment from artifact B to prior artifact A within 24 hours of B's release, with owner-coded reason and provenance | Deployment controller/change log; reliably observable only with an agreed rollback taxonomy and complete source. Final negative only after 24-hour coverage matures | Real deployment behavior, but rollback may be planned, unrelated, or precautionary; absence is not proof of health | Usually requires prior release; denial removes that exposure. Forward fixes are not rollbacks | Optional separate secondary target after source validation; do not infer from repeated SHA or `inactive` |
| Deployment cancellation | Explicit cancellation of the named request or running release job before terminal success/failure; record pre-start versus post-start and reason | Review/controller/cancellation journal at actual receipt; reason may be missing | Administrative or operational action, not proof the release would fail | Strongly affected by decisions, supersession and concurrency cancellation; denying is not a natural failure label | Separate competing state/selection variable, never positive primary label |
| Failed post-release health check | A prespecified environment-specific health predicate is violated during the first 30 minutes after a verified release side effect, using a versioned check and complete telemetry | Health collector with artifact/environment/exposure link; positive at first breach, negative only after complete horizon | Closer to running-service behavior; predicate failure need not imply incident severity or release causation | No target exposure after denial; observation can also depend on monitoring coverage | Stronger operational endpoint if available, but currently UNVERIFIED; prospective adoption needs new review |
| Post-deployment incident | An incident record meeting a fixed severity definition and beginning within 24 hours of verified release, linked to the service; release attribution recorded separately | Incident system and exposure history; occurrence horizon is 24 hours, adjudication may arrive later | Consequential service outcome; temporal association does not identify release causation | Denial changes exposure; incidents may still happen for unrelated reasons | Do not choose now: no source, severity contract, linkage or event-volume evidence |
| Release-delivery contract violation | A named artifact fails to become available at the intended registry/target within a prespecified owner-defined deadline after approval | Independent destination receipt/version verification plus delivery timestamps | Stronger evidence of failure to deliver than job conclusion alone; still not service health | Approval/no-start and execution failures can count under an explicitly new target; denial remains a different action | Possible replacement if an owner already records it; no silent conversion of missing job outcomes into failures |

The 30-minute and 24-hour horizons above are concrete screening proposals, not validated operating horizons. Keep these secondary outcomes separate; fix or replace their definitions with the owner **before** collecting evaluation outcomes. No searching for a horizon that improves apparent performance.

GitHub deployment objects represent requests; integrations supply deployment statuses. Thus a reported status needs validation against its producer and protected operation. A status of `inactive` is not a rollback or incident label. [Deployment semantics](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/deployments/deployments). Actions supplies job/step conclusions and timing, but those fields do not certify production health. [Job metadata](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/actions/workflow-jobs).

## Recommended outcome variable and denominator

Recommend **unsuccessful execution of one protected release job attempt**, conditional on a real release operation. A job containing only ordinary build/tests is ineligible for the primary release-stage cohort. A package publication and a service deployment are distinct strata; do not pool them as equivalent production deployments.

Proposed request identity: `(repository_id, immutable_artifact_id, environment_id, admission_request_id, run_id, run_attempt, protected_job_id)`. Store a verified artifact digest where artifacts exist; an immutable commit/workflow revision may identify a source-based release only if the owner can bind the actual executed content to it. A moving branch/tag alone is inadequate. If the protected job's ID is unavailable at the gate, preserve an immutable request-to-later-job join; never invent an early ID or timestamp.

For an approved, verified-started attempt:

- `Y_exec = 1`: terminal `failure`, or `timed_out` during execution, according to the fixed job contract.
- `Y_exec = 0`: terminal `success` for that contract.
- `Y_exec = null`: denial, gate timeout, no start, cancellation, supersession, skip, neutral/action-required/unknown state, missing result, or unresolved linkage. Store the reason separately. A gate-generated failed workflow never supplies `Y_exec=1`.

All admission requests remain in the denominator ledger. Report successive counts: requested, snapshot ready while held, approved/denied/unresolved, started/not started, terminal success/failure/timeout, cancelled and missing. A binary complete-case analysis estimates risk in the **approved, started, observed-terminal subset** unless additional missingness assumptions are justified. “Among approved releases” without that qualification would overstate coverage when non-starts or cancellations matter. See [selection analysis](selective-label-analysis.md).

Retain the original attempt's failure if a retry later succeeds. Reruns, multiple environments and repeated artifacts are linked dependent units, not additional independent projects. Prevent a retry from silently inheriting an earlier approval. If `continue-on-error`, masked exit codes, or asynchronous remote deployment mean job `success` does not implement the intended contract, either explicitly study that narrower job-execution result or reject the source for the intended endpoint. Do not fix labels using future information unavailable to the predictor.

Owner validation should review the workflow definition, all distinct failure/cancellation categories and a prespecified sample of ordinary successes as well as adverse cases. Record all disagreements, their resolutions and unresolvable cases. Failure subtype coding must be blind to predictions and distinguish setup/infrastructure, artifact delivery, deployment operation and post-operation checks. Keep all valid failures in the primary execution endpoint; subtype analysis must not quietly remove inconvenient errors. Record an independent delivery witness where the claim includes delivery.

## Decision and actuator

**APPROVE** permits the declared protected stage to proceed once *all* remaining prerequisites/protection rules pass. It does not force execution or guarantee delivery. **DENY** rejects the current pending admission and prevents that admission from authorizing execution. It does not revoke already deployed code, stop unprotected paths, or forever prohibit a new request for the same artifact. No canary action is proposed.

The normal decision-maker would be the owner's existing authorized release manager, maintainer, on-call reviewer or approved protection service; this must be recorded rather than assumed. Human review may combine technical risk with timing, compliance and business constraints outside our data. A predictor cannot replace those authorities merely by being accurate.

GitHub documents environment reviews and administrator bypass behavior. Technical enforcement therefore depends on the actual rules, review identity, bypass permissions and all paths to the side effect. [Reviewing deployments](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/how-tos/deploy/configure-and-manage-deployments/review-deployments). The pending-deployment review API operates at **run/environment scope**. One response may cover several jobs; our job-specific action is defensible only if that scope maps to exactly one declared job, or a separately reviewed bundle of jobs with a new outcome definition. [Review API](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/actions/workflow-runs#review-pending-deployments-for-a-workflow-run).

| Claim level | What the study would do | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction | Seal a risk score without displaying it or changing existing admission | Valid pre-admission context, future target, temporal evaluation and calibration within the selected cohort |
| Decision support | Show a score or recommendation to an authorized reviewer | Prediction evidence plus separately authorized user study of interpretation, decisions, burden and errors; displaying a score can change selection |
| Automatic enforcement | Exercise approve/deny through a validated gate | Separate explicit authorization, mechanism evidence, correct scope and identity, real cost/reward definitions and evaluation of actual decisions |

Automation is technically plausible for a narrow, owner-defined operational rule. Its appropriateness for the actual organization is UNKNOWN. Prediction is the first scientific gate; decision support and enforcement are not promised consequences.

## Is prediction alone worthwhile?

Potentially, if the protected operation has material failed-execution costs and context adds useful information beyond an owner rule and recent project/stage failure rate. It is not worthwhile as a numerical exercise if outcomes are nearly absent, failures are mostly unanticipated external outages, or nobody would use advance information.

If later authorized, compare a static/constant estimate, empirical/Bayesian/rolling stage rates and a contextual probability model with identical context cutoffs and label delays. Use chronological evaluation, artifact-group separation, calibration/proper scores, precision-recall at operationally tolerable alert burdens, and uncertainty across independent services/owners. Do not run these models now. A reproducible null result can be informative only with adequate precision and a meaningful cohort; “simple models suffice generally” would remain unjustified.

Safe-deployment analytics already exists: Gandalf studies cloud-scale deployment monitoring using operational signals. Our proposed contribution would need to distinguish **information available before this protected stage**, its selected population and outcome validity; adding a classifier to GitHub does not itself establish novelty. [Gandalf, Microsoft Research / NSDI 2020](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/an-intelligent-end-to-end-analytics-service-for-safe-deployment-in-large-scale-cloud-infrastructure/). This is a bounded novelty check, not a completed systematic literature review or publication guarantee.
